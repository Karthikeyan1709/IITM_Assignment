Business Problem Statement
BuildMax Infrastructure Ltd. is one of the largest engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) organizations in the region, responsible for delivering large-scale commercial buildings, transportation infrastructure, industrial facilities, residential developments, and public sector projects. Over the past several years, the company has experienced substantial growth due to increasing investments in urban development and infrastructure modernization programs. While project acquisition has continued to expand, executive leadership has become increasingly concerned about the rising frequency of project schedule delays and budget overruns across multiple business units.
Project delivery performance has emerged as a critical business challenge. Several high-profile projects have exceeded planned completion timelines by months and, in some cases, years. Cost overruns have significantly impacted project profitability, resulting in reduced margins, contractual disputes, regulatory challenges, and reputational risks. Internal reviews indicate that while individual project teams regularly monitor progress, many delays and cost escalations are only identified after significant impacts have already occurred. Leadership believes that early warning indicators may exist within project planning records, resource utilization data, contractor performance information, procurement activities, safety reports, environmental conditions, and financial tracking systems.
Multiple departments have raised concerns regarding project execution risks. Project managers report difficulties coordinating labor, subcontractors, and material suppliers. Procurement teams have identified increasing delays in material deliveries and vendor performance issues. Site engineers have observed productivity variations across different project phases and locations. Finance teams have reported growing discrepancies between planned and actual expenditures. Safety teams have identified incidents that contribute to work stoppages and schedule disruptions. Executive leadership believes that valuable insights may be hidden within historical project data and wants to better understand the factors contributing to project delays and budget overruns.
Currently, project monitoring relies heavily on periodic reporting and manual assessments. Leadership wants to transition toward a predictive project management approach capable of identifying projects that are at risk of delays or cost escalation before major impacts occur. The analytics team has been provided with historical project data collected from project management systems, procurement platforms, contractor management applications, financial systems, and site operations records. The dataset contains information related to project characteristics, labor utilization, contractor performance, procurement activities, safety incidents, financial metrics, environmental conditions, and several legacy administrative attributes inherited from previous systems. Management has intentionally not prescribed a specific analytical approach and expects the analytics team to investigate the available information, identify meaningful patterns, uncover factors associated with project delays and cost overruns, and recommend data-driven solutions that can improve project execution performance and profitability.
